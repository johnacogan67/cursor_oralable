## 2. How the jaw channel works

The pod sits on the chin, in the groove under the lower lip. Pharyngeal muscles that hold the airway open attach to the mandible. When the airway narrows, those muscles pull and the jaw moves. Larger motion means more respiratory effort. Pépin 2022 compared that amplitude with esophageal pressure, the invasive effort trace, during in-lab PSG.

Two inertial channels, as Sunrise states them:

| Sensor | What it sees | Event read |
|--------|----------------|------------|
| Accelerometer | Jaw drops (mouth opens) | Present in both obstructive and central events |
| Gyroscope | Jaw rocks at breathing rate (effort) | Obstructive: rock grows. Central: rock shrinks or stops |

Wake jaw motion is fast and irregular. Sleep motion sits near the breathing rate. REM keeps the motion, but size and timing wander. Arousals are short, sharp jerks. Cloud software turns the night into a report (their “Sunrise AHI”, sleep stages, position) for a clinician. It is an aid. It is not a substitute for full polysomnography (classification rule for QRS).

Company FAQ figures, not Oralable data: wake vs sleep sensitivity 88% and specificity 94% on 226 synced PSGs; REM sensitivity 83% and specificity 89% on 1,026 patients.

## 3. Claim discipline

- Say **De Novo** (sensor 1) or **510(k) cleared** (sensor 2, Sunrise Air). Do not copy their FAQ line “FDA approved.”
- Indication on the letters: home aid for **OSA evaluation** in adults **18+** with suspected sleep-breathing disorders. Li 2025 (episodic bruxism sensitivity; weak on tonic clench and on people without OSA) is a literature note, not this indication.
- Sensor 1 is motion only. SpO₂, pulse, and airflow arrive with sensor 2. Snore arrives with Sunrise Air.
- Oralable does not report Sunrise AHI. Chin jaw kinematics are not temple IR-DC. Not Dual A. Not a Beacon box item.
